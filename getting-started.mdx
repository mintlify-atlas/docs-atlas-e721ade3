SkillTanki has no sign-up flow, no installer, and no progression wall. From a cold start to the first shot takes a matter of seconds: open the link, type a name, pick a tank, and choose a battle. There is nothing else standing between you and the game.

## Start playing

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the game">
    Navigate to [mobile.skilltanki.workers.dev](https://mobile.skilltanki.workers.dev/) in any modern browser — desktop or mobile, no plugin required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter a call sign">
    Type any name you want to be known by in battle. No registration, no e-mail, no password — just a call sign.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose your tank">
    Pick a hull, a turret, and a paint from the garage. Every hull (Wasp through Mammoth), every turret (Smoky through Shaft), and all 101 paints are unlocked and free from the first second.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a mode and map, then battle">
    Select a game mode (Deathmatch, Team Deathmatch, Capture the Flag, Assault, or Explore Map) and a map from the full 91-map collection. Join an existing room from the battle list or create your own — then drive in.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Everything is unlocked from the first second — no grind, no progression ladder.
</Note>

## Join or create a room

There are two types of rooms, and you can create either from the same "New room" screen.

**Public rooms** appear in the shared battle list visible to everyone. Any player can see the room, pick a side, and join. This is the standard way to meet random opponents and fill a match quickly.

**Private rooms** are password-protected and marked with a lock icon in the battle list. Only players who know the password can enter. The password is never exposed anywhere — not in the shareable link, not in the room listing. If your connection drops or the page reloads mid-session, you return to your own tank without losing the score.

## Playing solo

When you create a room in Deathmatch mode you can choose to play solo against bots. The bot count is fully adjustable — dial it up for a challenge or all the way down to zero if you just want to drive around and learn the map geometry. Solo sessions run offline once loaded, which makes them a good option for trying out a new gun, practicing turret control, or warming up before joining a multiplayer room.
