Nine guns are nine different answers to the question "how exactly does the damage arrive." The difference between them is not a matter of raw numbers — it is the very mechanics of the shot. Choosing a turret means choosing a playstyle, and no turret is strictly better than another: each one excels when its mechanics fit the situation and falters when they don't.

## All nine turrets

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Smoky — Instant hit" icon="circle-dot">
    **Mechanic:** The shot lands instantly, exactly where the barrel points the moment you press fire. There is no projectile to watch — press the button and the hit (or miss) resolves immediately.

    **Profile:** Medium damage, medium reload rate, works effectively at any range. No leading required, no charge time, no special resource to manage.

    **Role:** The benchmark gun. Every other turret's trade-offs make sense once you know Smoky as the baseline. It is the most forgiving turret to pick up and the most honest one to measure your aim against, making it the natural starting point for learning maps and positioning.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ricochet — Bouncing projectile" icon="angles-right">
    **Mechanic:** The polar opposite of Smoky. A plasma bolt really flies at finite speed — you must lead a moving target, not just point at it. In return, the bolt **bounces off walls**, multiple times in a row.

    **Profile:** The only gun in the game that shoots around corners. A bolt fired at the right wall angle can strike an enemy hidden completely behind cover, without you ever exposing yourself.

    **Role:** Map geometry becomes your weapon. Players who learn the angles on a battlefield can land hits that seem impossible to the target. The higher the skill investment in geometry knowledge, the more Ricochet pays off — a high-ceiling gun that rewards patience and map study.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Railgun — Charged piercing beam" icon="bolt-lightning">
    **Mechanic:** Instant on release, like Smoky, but preceded by a **long charge phase** with a visibly growing glow on the muzzle. Everyone on the map can see the charge building. On release, the beam pierces everyone lined up along its path — each successive target takes less damage, but one well-aimed shot down a queue drops two or three tanks simultaneously.

    **Profile:** The biggest single-hit damage in the game. Also the longest penalty for a miss, and total helplessness during the charge — a charging Railgun cannot dodge, sprint, or react to a flanker.

    **Role:** A gun of cold calculation. Ideal for players who control the pace of an engagement — get the shot lined up before the enemy reaches you, punish grouped enemies, retreat to charge safely. The visible charge creates a mind game: the enemy knows a beam is coming; you know they know.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Twins — Rapid fire" icon="fire">
    **Mechanic:** A fast-firing double-barrel that alternates shots from two barrels, spitting slow plasma in a near-continuous stream. Each individual shot deals small damage.

    **Profile:** The aggregate damage-per-second at close range is competitive, but every individual bolt flies slowly enough to miss against a fast-moving Wasp at distance.

    **Role:** A close-range pressure gun. Keep your barrel on the enemy and the stream never really stops — it interrupts their aiming, forces them to evade, and accumulates damage quickly in confined spaces. Works best on narrow maps or in cramped corridors where keeping distance is impossible.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Thunder — Area explosion" icon="explosion">
    **Mechanic:** The shell flies to the target point and **detonates over an area**, dealing damage to everyone within range of the impact. A direct hit is not required — aiming at the wall beside a tank or the floor beneath it is often the correct play.

    **Profile:** Self-damage applies if you stand near your own blast, so positioning awareness is essential. On the other hand, it is the most forgiving gun for enemies who are moving — the splash catches targets even when the direct aim is slightly off.

    **Role:** Area denial and crowd control. In team modes, Thunder excels at forcing enemies out of chokepoints and punishing clusters. In Deathmatch, it rewards players who anticipate movement paths rather than tracking individual targets.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Firebird — Stream and burn" icon="fire-flame-curved">
    **Mechanic:** Not a shot but a **continuous stream** emitted while the trigger is held. Short effective range, powered by an energy bar that drains while firing. The stream sets the enemy on fire.

    **Profile:** A burning enemy **continues taking damage after leaving the stream** — the fire persists for several seconds. While burning, that enemy also takes increased damage from any source, including other guns and their own explosions.

    **Role:** "Ignite and finish." Drive into close range, lock the stream onto the enemy until they catch fire, then break off and let the burn and bonus damage do the rest. Pairs well with a teammate who can exploit the damage amplification. At range, Firebird is nearly useless; in close quarters, it is among the most punishing weapons in the game.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Freeze — Stream and control" icon="snowflake">
    **Mechanic:** Mechanically identical to Firebird — continuous stream, same range, same energy bar. The effect is the opposite: instead of igniting the target, Freeze **slows their reload significantly**.

    **Profile:** Lower damage output than Firebird. The trade is disruption: a frozen Railgun player cannot fire on schedule, a frozen Thunder cannot spam splash, a frozen Smoky is consistently late to respond.

    **Role:** A gun of control, not damage. Freeze is at its most powerful against turrets that depend on precise timing — Railgun, Thunder, Shaft. Lock a dangerous enemy in Freeze and let your teammates eliminate them while their response is delayed. In 1v1, it is a tempo-breaking tool that can turn a disadvantageous position into a survivable one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Isida — Stream, drain, and heal" icon="heart-pulse">
    **Mechanic:** The beam locks onto a single target within range. Against an **enemy**, it drains their health — a portion of what is drained returns to the Isida user as their own health. Against an **ally**, it heals them directly.

    **Profile:** Dual-purpose utility: offensive drain that sustains the Isida player, and a full-team healing function in co-operative play.

    **Role:** In team battles, Isida turns a player into a front-line medic. Pair Isida with a Titan or Mammoth hull and you create a durable, self-sustaining anchor that is extraordinarily difficult to remove from a key position. The drain function means Isida is also viable in Deathmatch, slowly outpacing opponents by recovering the damage they deal.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Shaft — Sniper" icon="crosshairs">
    **Mechanic:** Two distinct fire modes on one gun.

    - **Hip fire** — a quick jab that fires without entering scope mode. Rapid and functional in a brawl; you are never fully helpless at close range.
    - **Scoped mode** — hold the aim button: the camera drops to the barrel and zooms like optics, the tank stands completely still, and the shot charges. The longer the hold, the harder the charge — at full charge, it delivers Railgun-level damage at the longest effective range in the game. While scoped, **everyone on the map sees the laser dot**.

    **Profile:** The most range-versatile gun in the game. The sniper penalty — immobility while aiming and the visible laser — creates the same mind-game dynamic as the Railgun charge, but at greater distance.

    **Role:** A duel of attention. Shaft rewards players who control map vision: set up on a high-ground position, use the laser to threaten the enemy's movement, and punish static targets with charged shots. Hip fire gives an escape option when the enemy gets close. The visible laser is both your power and your vulnerability — experienced opponents will track the dot back to its source.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  Start with Smoky to learn the maps, then experiment with Ricochet or Shaft once you're comfortable with positioning.
</Tip>
