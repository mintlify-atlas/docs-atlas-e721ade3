In the 2010 original, supplies were bought with crystals — whoever had the thicker wallet could fire double damage without pause, turning a mechanical advantage into a financial one. SkillTanki removes that entirely. Every supply is **infinite and free for every player**. The only limiter is a cooldown after use, and that cooldown is identical for everyone. Supplies stopped being merchandise and became tactics: the question is no longer "did you buy it?" but "did you press it at the right moment?"

## The five supplies

| Key | Supply | Effect |
|-----|--------|--------|
| `1` | **Repair kit** | Instantly restores the tank to full health. Best used when health is critically low but cover is available to escape while it activates. |
| `2` | **Double armour** | Halves all incoming damage for its duration. Effective for pushing through a contested chokepoint or absorbing a Railgun charge you cannot dodge. |
| `3` | **Double damage** | Strengthens your shots for its duration. Deliberately tuned weaker than the 2010 original — even at full double damage, a Railgun will not one-shot a medium hull. A kill still requires a sequence of decisions. |
| `4` | **Nitro** | Delivers a noticeable boost to speed and acceleration for its duration. Tuned stronger than the original and available to everyone equally, making high-speed positioning a skill rather than a privilege. |
| `5` | **Nuclear energy** | Instantly readies the gun regardless of its current reload state, and speeds up reload for its duration. Most powerful on slow-cycling guns like Railgun and Thunder. |

## Balance implications

All five supplies operate on the same cooldown system for every player — there is no way to carry more charges, reduce cooldown time, or purchase additional uses. The playing field is flat by design.

**Double damage** was deliberately tuned below the 2010 original's values. In the classic, a boosted Railgun could one-shot nearly anything. Here, even the heaviest boost leaves the target alive if their health is reasonably high. A kill remains a series of decisions — positioning, timing, supply sequencing — not a single lucky button press at the right moment.

**Nitro** was deliberately tuned above the original's values. Faster tanks create more interesting positional dynamics: flanks open up, flag runs become genuine escapes, and the tactical geography of a map matters more when every player can briefly exploit it. Speed became a positioning skill, not a paid privilege.

The outcome of every battle is verified server-side, the same impartial referee for everyone — no client-side tricks can alter supply timings or cooldown states.

<Note>
  Supplies are activated with keys 1–5. There is no cooldown penalty for unused supplies — only for supplies you have activated.
</Note>

<Tip>
  In CTF and Assault, Nitro (`4`) on the flag carrier is often the difference between a successful return and a kill. Save it for the moment you pick up the flag, not the moment you are already being hit.
</Tip>
